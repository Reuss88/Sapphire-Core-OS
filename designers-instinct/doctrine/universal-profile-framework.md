# Universal Profile Framework

## Status

Permanent doctrine for knowledge-bearing commercial profiles across Sapphire Core OS.

## Governing principle

Every commercially relevant thing must be capable of accumulating structured, evidence-backed institutional knowledge.

Sapphire must not rely on fragmented notes, memory, browser tabs or individual expertise when evaluating an acquisition, counterparty, asset, contract or market.

## Dominant commercial question

What is this thing, what is it worth, what affects that value, what do we know, what remains uncertain, and what must be verified before action?

## Universal profile doctrine

Sapphire uses one **Universal Profile Framework** with typed profiles rather than unrelated profile systems.

Profiles may represent:

- commodities and commodity grades;
- houses, land and commercial property;
- boats, vehicles, aircraft and machinery;
- watches, jewellery, art and collectables;
- companies, people, banks and professional firms;
- mines, refineries, warehouses, ports and facilities;
- contracts, mandates and financial instruments;
- deals, shipments and acquisition candidates;
- countries, regions, routes and markets;
- any future asset or commercial object approved by Governance.

## Core profile capabilities

Every profile type inherits:

- canonical identity and aliases;
- classification and ownership;
- commercial summary;
- current status;
- structured attributes;
- pricing and valuation history;
- evidence and source provenance;
- confidence and verification state;
- risks, red flags and unknowns;
- relationships and knowledge graph links;
- documents and communications;
- timeline and material events;
- lessons learned;
- AI summary and recommendations;
- actions, missions, opportunities and deals;
- audit history.

Type-specific schemas extend this core.

## Acquisition profile

An acquisition profile is a profile used to evaluate whether Sapphire should source, buy, list, broker, hold, sell or avoid an asset or commercial position.

It must support, where applicable:

- exact specification, grade, condition or configuration;
- location, custody and legal title;
- supply and demand conditions;
- comparable transactions;
- replacement cost and liquidation value;
- market, broker and counterparty pricing;
- fees, taxes, logistics, finance and holding costs;
- authenticity and verification evidence;
- ownership and encumbrance checks;
- downside, upside and sensitivity analysis;
- target buy, list and sell ranges;
- minimum margin and commission thresholds;
- negotiation history;
- acquisition thesis;
- exit routes;
- confidence-adjusted valuation.

A price recommendation without source, timestamp, method, currency, unit and confidence is not authoritative.

## Knowledge completeness

Profiles must make missing knowledge visible. Unknown, unverified and disputed facts must not be rendered as confirmed truth.

Profile completeness is type-specific and should identify the information still required for a reliable commercial decision.

## Pricing doctrine

Broker pricing must be evidence-led.

Sapphire must preserve:

- quoted prices;
- observed market prices;
- completed transaction prices;
- comparable evidence;
- valuation method;
- adjustments;
- source reliability;
- time validity;
- currency and unit normalisation;
- confidence;
- Director overrides and rationale.

Historical evidence must remain available after recommendations change.

## Ownership

Profiles owns canonical knowledge objects and typed profile data.

Network owns relationship identity and authority for people and organisations. Deals owns execution. Documents owns files. Inbox owns communications. Intelligence owns analysis and synthesis. Actions owns required work.

These systems link to the profile rather than duplicating its knowledge.

## Implementation contract

The implementation must support:

- a stable profile identity layer;
- profile-type registry and versioned type schemas;
- common relational fields plus controlled type-specific attributes;
- evidence and provenance tables;
- valuation observations and valuation runs;
- temporal history rather than destructive overwrite;
- profile relationships and graph edges;
- type-aware RLS;
- RPCs for profile snapshots, completeness, valuation and evidence retrieval;
- triggers for material-change events and stale knowledge;
- search and semantic retrieval without bypassing permissions;
- audit of source, verification, override and recommendation changes.

A single ungoverned JSON blob is not an acceptable substitute for the Universal Profile Framework.
